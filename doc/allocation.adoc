[#webrtc-allocation]
== Memory

`basic_peer_connection<Executor, Allocator>` and
`basic_data_channel<Executor, Allocator>` take an allocator as a standard
container does; `peer_connection` and `data_channel` are the two with the
default executor and `std::allocator<std::byte>`. `make` takes the allocator,
and a connection's channels use its own:

* *the object's state* is allocated through the object's allocator, on the
  executor: the connection and its channels, the queues of candidates and of
  incoming channels, and the text of descriptions, candidates and labels,
  which the outputs carry, `session_description` and `ice_candidate`
  holding their strings in it;
* *an operation's state* is allocated through its completion handler's
  associated allocator, as Asio's operations are, so a program that binds an
  allocator to its handlers, or uses one with its coroutines, controls that
  memory too;
* *what runs on libdatachannel's threads* allocates through the standard
  allocator only: the copy an event carries to the executor, and the post
  that carries it. The object's allocator may be a resource used only on the
  executor, and it is never touched from another thread;
* *what the platform holds is its own:* natively, libdatachannel's objects
  and the messages in its queues, and the copies its C API makes when a
  message is read (<<webrtc-native-cost,what reading a message costs>>).

The allocator outlives the io_context's pending handlers, since a posted
completion may hold the state until it runs. None of the types is shareable
between threads: each is used on its executor.
