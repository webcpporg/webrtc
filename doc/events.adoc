[#webrtc-events]
== Events

What the W3C delivers as an event, webrtc delivers as the completion of a
wait the program starts, and starts again when it wants the next one, as an
acceptor's `async_accept`. Every handler then runs on the object's executor,
and nothing outlives what the program holds:

[cols="2,3",options="header"]
|===
| The W3C's event | The wait

| `icecandidate` | `peer_connection::async_wait_ice_candidate`
| `connectionstatechange` | `peer_connection::async_wait_state_change`
| `iceconnectionstatechange` | `peer_connection::async_wait_ice_connection_state_change`
| `datachannel` | `peer_connection::async_accept_data_channel`
| `open` | `data_channel::async_wait_open`
| `close` | `data_channel::async_wait_close`
| `bufferedamountlow` | `data_channel::async_wait_buffered_amount_low`
| `message` | `data_channel::async_receive`
|===

[#webrtc-events-policies]
=== While no wait is pending

An event can happen while the program has no wait of its kind pending, and
each kind says what becomes of it, so that none is lost without a rule:

* *A state coalesces.* A wait for a state change completes with the current
  state once it differs from the one the previous wait completed with, so a
  program that waits late sees the latest state, not every step to it.
* *Candidates queue* up to `options::max_queued_candidates`, 64 by default,
  in order; past it a candidate is dropped from the queue and stays in
  `local_description()`. The end of gathering has a place of its own past
  the bound, so it is never dropped.
* *Incoming channels queue* up to `options::max_queued_channels`, 16 by
  default; a channel past it is closed at once, as a connection past a
  listen backlog is refused.
* *Messages are kept* from the channel's creation until `async_receive`
  reads them (<<webrtc-channels-receiving>>), so a channel accepted late, or
  handed from one owner to another, loses nothing it received before.
* `open`, `close` and the buffered amount are states: a wait started after
  the channel opened, closed, or fell under its threshold completes at once,
  posted.

*One wait of a kind at a time.* A second wait of the same kind on the same
object completes with `already_started`, and the first stays pending. A
cancelled wait (<<webrtc-cancellation>>) consumes no event: the next wait
gets what it would have got.

[#webrtc-events-work]
=== Waits and the `io_context`

A pending operation is outstanding work on the object's executor and on its
handler's, as an Asio operation is: natively, `run()` does not return while
a candidate or a state change is awaited. A program that starts its waits
again after each completion keeps `run()` running until it closes the
connection, and closing is how it ends. What arrives while no wait is pending
is no work: a queued candidate or message does not keep `run()` alive.

[#webrtc-events-close]
=== Closing

`peer_connection::close()` is the W3C's `close()`: every pending operation of
the connection completes with `operation_aborted`, `state()` reads `closed`,
and no state change is delivered for it. Its channels then close, and their
pending operations complete with `error::closed`. A wait started on a closed
connection completes with `error::closed`.

`data_channel::close()` completes the channel's pending operations with
`operation_aborted`, except `async_wait_close`, which completes once the
channel is closed. When a channel closes for another reason, the peer, its
connection, its transport, its pending operations complete with
`error::closed` and `async_wait_close` completes without an error: that is
how a program learns that the other side is gone.

Destroying a connection or a channel closes it first. Natively the
destructor then waits until libdatachannel's callbacks in flight for it have
returned, so nothing of it is touched afterwards (<<webrtc-native>>).
