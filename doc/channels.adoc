[#webrtc-channels]
== Data channels

A `data_channel` carries messages between two peers over their connection,
each message whole, text or binary. A connection holds any number of them,
each with its own label and its own reliability.

[#webrtc-channels-making]
=== Making one

One peer creates a channel with `create_data_channel(label, init)` and the
other accepts it with `async_accept_data_channel` once the connection is up.
`data_channel_init` is the W3C's `RTCDataChannelInit`: `ordered`, at most one
of `max_packet_life_time` and `max_retransmits` for a channel that may drop
messages, a `protocol`, and `negotiated` with an `id` for a channel both
peers create themselves with the same id, which neither announces and
neither accepts. `create_data_channel` returns a `result`: both reliability
limits at once, `negotiated` without an `id`, the id 65535, or a label or
protocol past 65,535 bytes fail with `type_error`; a closed connection with
`invalid_state`.

A channel is movable and not copyable; it belongs to whoever holds it, and
its executor's context outlives it. `ready_state()`, `label()`, `id()` and
`buffered_amount()` read what the W3C's attributes of the same names read.
`async_wait_open` completes once the channel is open, at once when it already
is, and with `error::closed` when it closed before it opened.

[#webrtc-channels-receiving]
=== Receiving

`async_receive(buffer, token)` appends the next message, whole, to the
caller's dynamic buffer, Asio's first version (`dynamic_buffer` of a string
or a vector), and completes with its size and whether it was
text. The buffer outlives the operation. A message larger than the room left
in the buffer (its `max_size()` less its `size()`) is not read: the
operation completes with `no_buffer_space` and the message stays queued, for
a later `async_receive` with a larger buffer.

Messages are kept from the moment the channel exists until they are read, in
the order they came. *Natively that queue is libdatachannel's*, of 1,024
messages: past it libdatachannel stops reading from the network and the peer
is slowed down, a flow control that never fails. But the thread that waits
for room is the one that delivers every channel of the connection, so *a
channel nobody reads stalls the connection's other channels* once 1,024 of
its messages wait. A program reads every channel it holds, or closes it.
Closing or destroying a channel discards what it holds unread, as the W3C
discards what arrives once a channel left `open`, and releases the others;
messages that arrived before the peer closed the channel are still
delivered, then `async_receive` completes with `error::closed`.

[#webrtc-channels-sending]
=== Sending

`send(buffers)` and `send_text(text)` queue one message and return at once:
nothing, or why the message was not queued, as an `error_code`.
`async_send` and `async_send_text` do the same, and complete once the message
is queued, at once, posted, for a program that composes operations. A send
fails, and the channel stays open, when:

* the channel is not open, `invalid_state`;
* the message is larger than the smaller of `options::max_message_size`,
  256 KiB by default, and the limit the peer announced, `message_too_large`;
* queueing it would take the buffered amount past
  `options::max_buffered_amount`, 16 MiB by default,
  `buffered_amount_exceeded`;
* natively, a text holds a zero byte, `operation_not_supported`, since
  libdatachannel takes a text as a zero-terminated string.
